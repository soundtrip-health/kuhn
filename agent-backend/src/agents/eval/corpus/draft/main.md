# MIRAS-T2D: Cardiovascular Outcomes with Mirasatide in Type 2 Diabetes

## Abstract
Type 2 diabetes with established cardiovascular disease carries a high risk of
major adverse cardiovascular events. We evaluated mirasatide, a twice-daily
glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist, in 2,482 patients randomized 1:1 to
mirasatide or placebo with a median follow-up of 2.9 years.

## Results
Mirasatide reduced the rate of the primary composite endpoint — cardiovascular
death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, and nonfatal stroke — compared with
placebo. The composite occurred in 98 of 1,240 patients (7.9%) receiving
mirasatide versus 120 of 1,242 (9.7%) receiving placebo (hazard ratio 0.82,
95% CI 0.71–0.94; p=0.008).

Hospitalization for heart failure was also reduced in the mirasatide group
compared with placebo (hazard ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.61–0.90; p<0.05).

All-cause mortality was numerically lower with mirasatide (hazard ratio 0.79,
95% CI 0.57–1.10; p=0.16), a trend consistent with a survival benefit that
may become significant with longer follow-up.

Glycemic control improved substantially: mean HbA1c fell by 1.4 percentage
points at week 52 in the mirasatide group. Nausea was more common with
mirasatide (23% versus 14%; p=0.002).

## Discussion
These findings establish mirasatide as superior to all existing
cardioprotective therapies in patients with type 2 diabetes and demonstrate
that glycemic and cardiovascular benefit can be achieved with a single agent.
The dual reduction in MACE and heart-failure hospitalization suggests a
mechanism beyond glucose lowering.
