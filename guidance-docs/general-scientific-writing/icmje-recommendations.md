# ICMJE Recommendations for the Conduct, Reporting, Editing, and Publication of Scholarly Work in Medical Journals

> **Kuhn knowledge card.** Canonical source: https://www.icmje.org/ (International Committee of Medical Journal Editors). Source access/license: freely accessible online; ICMJE text is copyrighted — do not reproduce it verbatim. This card is a Kuhn-authored summary — cite and consult the canonical source for authoritative text.

## Scope

The ICMJE Recommendations (historically the "Uniform Requirements for Manuscripts")
are the de facto global standard for ethical conduct and reporting in medical,
biomedical, and health-sciences publishing. They apply to original research
articles, reviews, case reports, and editorials submitted to any of the thousands
of journals that follow ICMJE — not just the handful of member journals. The
Recommendations cover four broad areas: roles and responsibilities (authors,
contributors, reviewers, editors, publishers), publishing and editorial issues
(corrections, retractions, overlapping publication, embargoes), manuscript
preparation, and clinical trial registration. Updated roughly annually.

## Key requirements

**Authorship — all four criteria must be met.** An author must have (1) made a
substantial contribution to the conception or design of the work, or to acquiring,
analyzing, or interpreting its data; (2) drafted the manuscript or revised it
critically for important intellectual content; (3) approved the final version to
be published; and (4) agreed to be accountable for all aspects of the work,
including investigating and resolving questions about its accuracy or integrity.
Meeting only some criteria (e.g., funding acquisition, general supervision, or
data collection alone) earns acknowledgment, not authorship. Everyone who meets
all four criteria must be listed — omitting a qualifying contributor (ghost
authorship) is as much a violation as adding an unqualified one (gift/guest
authorship). Author order is the coauthors' joint decision; the corresponding
author handles journal communication and ensures administrative requirements
are met.

**Disclosure of conflicts of interest.** All participants — authors, reviewers,
editors — must disclose relationships and activities that could bias, or be
perceived to bias, the work: financial ties (employment, consultancies, stock,
honoraria, patents, paid testimony), and relevant non-financial relationships.
Authors use the ICMJE uniform disclosure form. Funding sources and the funder's
role in design, analysis, and the decision to publish must be stated.

**AI tools cannot be authors.** Chatbots and other AI tools do not qualify for
authorship because they cannot take accountability. Use of AI in drafting or
analysis must be disclosed in the manuscript (cover letter, acknowledgments, or
methods as appropriate), and authors remain fully responsible for AI-assisted
content, including checking it for plagiarism and fabricated citations.

**Clinical trial registration.** Trials must be registered in a public registry
(e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov or another WHO-network primary registry) before or at
first patient enrollment as a condition of publication. Reports should include
the registration identifier and follow the registered protocol; data-sharing
statements are required for clinical trial reports.

**Publication ethics.** Duplicate submission and redundant (overlapping)
publication are prohibited; secondary publication is acceptable only under
defined conditions with full transparency and cross-reference. Errors require
corrections; fraud or major unreliability requires retraction. Protect patient
privacy: identifying details need written informed consent for publication.
Research must comply with ethical oversight (e.g., IRB/ethics committee review,
Declaration of Helsinki principles).

**Manuscript conventions.** ICMJE endorses structured abstracts, reporting
guidelines appropriate to study design, and its long-standing reference style
(the basis of the NLM/"Vancouver" format) with references numbered in order of
citation.

## How to apply when writing

- Settle authorship early. Before drafting, map each contributor against the
  four criteria; list qualifying contributors as authors and everyone else
  (funders, supervisors, technicians, medical writers) in the acknowledgments
  with their permission.
- In the title page and declarations section, include: all author affiliations,
  the corresponding author, funding statement with the funder's role, a
  conflict-of-interest statement for every author (even if "none"), ethics
  approval reference, and trial registration number where applicable.
- In Methods, state ethics approval and consent procedures, and cite the trial
  registry ID for trials. If AI tools assisted with writing or analysis,
  disclose how, and verify every AI-suggested citation exists.
- Follow the target journal's ICMJE-based instructions for abstract structure
  and reference format; number references in citation order unless the journal
  says otherwise.
- Include a data-sharing statement for clinical trial manuscripts (what will be
  shared, when, how, and under what conditions).

## Common pitfalls

- Listing a department head or funder as an author when they meet none (or only
  some) of the four criteria — that belongs in acknowledgments.
- Omitting a professional medical writer or statistician who actually meets all
  four criteria, or failing to acknowledge writing assistance at all.
- Declaring "no conflicts" reflexively instead of disclosing disclosable
  relationships and letting editors and readers judge relevance.
- Registering a clinical trial after enrollment began, then discovering the
  journal will not consider the manuscript.
- Splitting one study into multiple overlapping papers ("salami slicing")
  without cross-referencing, risking redundant-publication findings.
- Treating final approval as a formality — every author is accountable for the
  whole paper, not just their section.
