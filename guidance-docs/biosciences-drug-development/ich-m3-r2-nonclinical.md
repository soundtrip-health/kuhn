# ICH M3(R2) — Nonclinical Safety Studies to Support Clinical Trials

> **Kuhn knowledge card.** Canonical source: https://www.fda.gov/media/71542/download (FDA "Guidance for Industry" edition of the ICH M3(R2) harmonized guideline). Source access/license: free; the FDA-issued guidance is a U.S. government work in the public domain (the parallel ICH-database text is freely downloadable from ICH). This card is a Kuhn-authored summary — cite and consult the canonical source for authoritative text.

## Scope

ICH M3(R2), "Nonclinical Safety Studies for the Conduct of Human Clinical Trials and Marketing Authorization for Pharmaceuticals," is the harmonized multidisciplinary guideline defining **which nonclinical safety studies are needed, and when, to support each stage of human testing** — from first-in-human (FIH) dosing through Phase 2/3 and marketing authorization. It covers small-molecule pharmaceuticals; biotechnology-derived products follow ICH S6(R1) for product-specific aspects, but M3(R2) timing principles still apply. It addresses safety pharmacology, toxicokinetics, acute and repeat-dose toxicity, genotoxicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, immunotoxicity, phototoxicity, abuse liability, metabolite qualification, combination toxicity, and special designs such as exploratory clinical trials (microdosing). It is the backbone reference for the nonclinical sections of INDs, CTAs, investigator's brochures, and CTD Module 2.4/2.6 summaries.

## Key requirements

Paraphrased core principles:

- **Timing is staged to clinical exposure.** The nonclinical package grows with the clinical program: enough safety data must exist *before* each phase to characterize toxic effects, choose a safe starting dose, and identify parameters for clinical monitoring — not everything is needed up front.
- **Repeat-dose toxicity duration must cover clinical duration.** Clinical trials are generally supported by repeat-dose toxicity studies in two species (one non-rodent) of at least equal duration to the clinical trial, up to defined maximums: studies of up to 2 weeks support 2-week trials; longer trials require toxicity studies of matching length up to 6 months in rodents and 9 months in non-rodents, which also support marketing for chronic-use drugs.
- **Before first-in-human dosing** the expected minimum package is:
  - safety pharmacology core battery — cardiovascular (including hERG/QT assessment per ICH S7A/S7B), respiratory, and CNS;
  - repeat-dose toxicity studies in two species by the intended clinical route;
  - genotoxicity — a gene-mutation assay before FIH, with the full battery per ICH S2(R1) before Phase 2;
  - toxicokinetic and pharmacokinetic/ADME data (in vitro metabolism and protein binding, systemic exposure in the toxicity species);
  - local-tolerance evaluation, usually folded into the general toxicity studies.
- **High-dose selection** in toxicity studies should be justified — by a maximum tolerated dose, a large exposure multiple (e.g., ~50-fold clinical exposure), saturation of exposure, or a limit dose — rather than defaulting to the largest feasible dose.
- **Reproductive toxicity is phased.**
  - Embryo-fetal development (EFD) studies before enrolling women of childbearing potential in larger or longer trials, with interim provisions for early trials that use pregnancy-prevention precautions.
  - Fertility and early embryonic development studies before Phase 3.
  - Pre- and postnatal development studies before marketing authorization.
  - Requirements for early inclusion of women of childbearing potential differ by region — the guideline harmonizes the general logic and flags the remaining regional differences.
- **Carcinogenicity studies** (ICH S1 series) are generally needed only for marketing of drugs given continuously for ≥6 months or intermittently for chronic conditions — not for most clinical-trial support — unless there is specific cause for concern.
- **Metabolites** disproportionately present in humans (roughly >10% of drug-related exposure and at much higher exposure than seen in animal studies) may need dedicated nonclinical characterization ("MIST" considerations).
- **Exploratory clinical trials** (e.g., microdose studies, single sub-therapeutic doses) may proceed with reduced nonclinical packages, scaled to the approach, enabling earlier human data with less animal testing.
- **Other studies as triggered:**
  - immunotoxicity per ICH S8 when signals from the general toxicity studies or the pharmacology warrant it;
  - phototoxicity assessment when the molecule absorbs light in the relevant range and distributes to skin or eye;
  - abuse-liability studies for CNS-active drugs;
  - nonclinical combination toxicity studies when two entities lack adequate individual or combined precedent.
- **Recovery and reversibility** of toxic effects should be assessed when appropriate, and the guideline discourages studies that add no value — consistent with 3Rs (reduce/refine/replace) principles.

## How to apply when writing

- In IND/CTA nonclinical summaries and investigator's brochures, organize the argument as M3(R2) does: for each proposed clinical phase and duration, state the supporting studies (species, route, duration, GLP status) and show that study duration ≥ clinical duration and that exposure margins are adequate.
- Justify the FIH starting dose explicitly: cite the NOAEL (no-observed-adverse-effect level) from the most sensitive relevant species, the conversion to a human-equivalent dose, and the safety factor applied — and reconcile with exposure (AUC/Cmax) margins, not just mg/kg.
- Tabulate the package against the guideline: a study-by-study table (study type, species, duration, key findings, NOAEL, exposure multiple) is the fastest way for reviewers to check M3(R2) coverage.
- State genotoxicity and safety-pharmacology results before any efficacy narrative — these gate human dosing.
- When a standard study is absent, don't stay silent: cite the M3(R2) provision that makes it unnecessary at this stage (e.g., carcinogenicity deferred to marketing application; EFD staged relative to enrollment of women of childbearing potential) and note any agreed regulatory advice.
- For manuscripts describing IND-enabling programs, use M3(R2) vocabulary (GLP repeat-dose toxicity, core battery, toxicokinetics, NOAEL, exposure margin) so the work is legible to regulatory readers.

## Common pitfalls

- Claiming clinical-trial support from toxicity studies shorter than the proposed dosing duration, or from a single species.
- Reporting only mg/kg dose multiples instead of exposure (AUC/Cmax) margins.
- Ignoring human-specific metabolites that exceed the qualification threshold.
- Treating the guideline as a rigid checklist — high-dose selection, recovery arms, and triggered studies (immunotoxicity, phototoxicity, abuse liability) all require case-by-case scientific justification in the text.
- Forgetting the staged reproductive-toxicity logic when trials enroll women of childbearing potential.
- Confusing M3(R2) (timing/scope) with the study-design guidelines it points to (S7A/S7B, S2(R1), S5, S6(R1), S8) — cite the right document for design details.
