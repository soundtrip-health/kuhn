# IUPAC Gold Book — Compendium of Chemical Terminology

> **Kuhn knowledge card.** Canonical source: https://goldbook.iupac.org/ (International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry). Source access/license: free online access; definitions are IUPAC-copyrighted, so paraphrase rather than copy. This card is a Kuhn-authored summary — cite and consult the canonical source for authoritative text.

## Scope

The Gold Book is IUPAC's online Compendium of Chemical Terminology: several thousand
standardized definitions of chemistry terms, compiled from IUPAC recommendations
published in *Pure and Applied Chemistry* and the other IUPAC "Color Books". It is the
arbiter of what a chemical term officially means. It applies to any chemistry
manuscript — organic, inorganic, physical, analytical, polymer, biochemistry — plus
adjacent fields (materials, pharma, environmental) whenever a term of art appears in a
title, abstract, methods section, or glossary. It is a terminology reference, not a
nomenclature manual: for systematically *naming* compounds it points to the Blue Book
(organic nomenclature), Red Book (inorganic), Purple Book (polymers), and for quantities
and units the Green Book.

## Key requirements

- **One term, one meaning.** Each entry gives the IUPAC-recommended definition, the
  source recommendation it came from, and cross-references. When a term has been
  redefined over time the entry tracks versions — cite the definition version/date when
  precision matters. Entries have stable identifiers and DOIs.
- **Deprecated and discouraged usage is flagged.** The Gold Book marks obsolete terms and
  common misuses. Distinctions it enforces include:
  - *molar mass* (mass per amount of substance, g/mol) vs *molecular weight* (relative
    molar mass, dimensionless);
  - *amount of substance* as the quantity name — not "number of moles";
  - *specific* reserved for "per unit mass" and *molar* for "per amount of substance"
    in quantity names.
- **Precise physical-organic vocabulary.** Terms like *nucleophilicity* vs *basicity*,
  *rate constant* vs *rate coefficient*, *transition state* vs *transition structure*,
  *tautomerism*, *aromaticity*, *hapticity*, and the stereodescriptors (*R/S*, *E/Z*,
  *cis/trans*, *syn/anti*) carry exact definitions; loose use invites reviewer correction.
- **Quantities and units follow the Green Book / SI.** Definitions embed correct quantity
  symbols, units, and typography (equilibrium constants, pH, ionic strength), consistent
  with ISO and BIPM conventions.
- **Concept boundaries matter.** Entries define working concepts — *yield*, *conversion*,
  *selectivity*, *catalyst*, *limiting reagent* — with limits: a *catalyst* increases the
  reaction rate without being consumed and without altering the overall thermodynamics;
  calling a stoichiometric additive a catalyst contradicts the definition.
- **Nomenclature conventions live in the Color Books but are anchored here.** Use IUPAC
  systematic or IUPAC-sanctioned trivial names on first mention (acetic acid and toluene
  are acceptable retained names). Locants, italicized prefixes (*o-*, *m-*, *p-*,
  *tert-*, *N-*), and multiplying prefixes follow Blue/Red Book rules.

## How to apply when writing

- When drafting or reviewing, check every load-bearing technical term in the title,
  abstract, and definitions section against the Gold Book; align with the recommended
  term or explicitly justify divergence.
- Prefer the recommended term over the colloquial one: "molar mass" (with g/mol),
  "amount of substance" (or reword to "n(X) = 2.0 mmol"), "mass fraction" instead of an
  ambiguous "concentration" when mass fraction is what was measured.
- Define specialized or field-local terms once, citing the Gold Book entry (stable DOI)
  so reviewers can verify the intended sense.
- Use the typography that flows from the definitions: italic quantity symbols (*c*, *T*,
  *K*), upright unit symbols (mol dm⁻³, kPa), italic stereo- and locant prefixes in
  compound names.
- When a term is deprecated (e.g., "normality" for concentration), replace it with the
  recommended quantity; in methods reproducing old protocols, give the modern equivalent
  alongside the historical term.
- For compound names, apply Blue/Red Book nomenclature and reserve the Gold Book for
  conceptual terms; if a name is software-generated, sanity-check the parent chain,
  locants, and stereodescriptors.

## Common pitfalls

- Using "molecular weight" with units of g/mol — report *molar mass* (g/mol) or
  *relative molecular mass* (unitless), not a hybrid.
- Writing "moles" as if it were the quantity ("the number of moles was measured")
  instead of amount of substance.
- Calling a reagent a catalyst when it is consumed stoichiometrically, or conflating
  conversion, yield, and selectivity in results tables.
- Roman/italic errors: unit symbols italicized, or stereo/locant prefixes (*tert*-,
  *N*-, *o*-) set upright.
- Citing "the Gold Book" without the entry identifier or version when the definition has
  changed across IUPAC recommendations.
- Treating the Gold Book as a nomenclature generator — it defines terms; systematic
  names come from the Blue/Red/Purple Book rules.
