# Reporting guidelines (CONSORT / STROBE / PRISMA / ARRIVE / SPIRIT)

Checklists for transparent, complete reporting. Most health-sciences journals
require adherence to the appropriate guideline. Use the right one for the study
type; when in doubt, check the **EQUATOR Network** (https://www.equator-network.org/).

| Study type | Guideline | Source |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Randomized controlled trial | **CONSORT** | https://www.consort-statement.org/ |
| Observational cohort / case-control / cross-sectional | **STROBE** | https://www.strobe-statement.org/ |
| Systematic review / meta-analysis | **PRISMA** (2020) | https://www.prisma-statement.org/ |
| Animal / preclinical research | **ARRIVE** (2.0) | https://arriveguidelines.org/ |
| Clinical trial protocol | **SPIRIT** | https://www.spirit-statement.org/ |

**Using a guideline:** identify the one matching your study type; read the checklist
*before* drafting (it informs what sections the document needs); cross-reference
section-by-section while drafting; run a final compliance pass before submission
(Reviewer scope). Incomplete reporting is the most common reason a methodologically
sound study is rejected or down-weighted in meta-analyses. If a project needs the
full guideline expanded with project-specific annotations, that annotated version
belongs in the project's `guidance/`.

**CONSORT** — RCTs (parallel-group, crossover, cluster, pragmatic). Extensions:
CONSORT-harms (safety), CONSORT-NPT (non-pharmacologic), CONSORT-EHEALTH,
CONSORT-PRO. Key sections: title/abstract, introduction, methods (design,
participants, interventions, outcomes, sample size, randomization, blinding,
statistical methods), results (participant flow, recruitment, baseline data,
outcomes, ancillary analyses, harms), discussion, other information.

**STROBE** — observational studies making inferences about associations or causal
effects. Extensions: RECORD (routinely collected data / EHRs), STROBE-MR, STROBE-ME.
Key sections: title/abstract, introduction, methods (design, setting, participants,
variables, data sources, bias, study size, quantitative variables, statistical
methods), results (participants, descriptive data, outcome data, main results, other
analyses), discussion (key results, limitations, interpretation, generalizability),
funding.

**PRISMA** — 27-item checklist + 4-phase flow diagram for systematic reviews,
meta-analyses, scoping reviews (PRISMA-ScR). Extensions: PRISMA-P (protocols),
PRISMA-IPD, PRISMA-NMA, PRISMA-CI. Key sections: title/abstract, introduction
(rationale, objectives), methods (eligibility, info sources, search, selection, data
collection, outcomes, risk of bias, synthesis methods), results (study selection,
characteristics, risk of bias, effect sizes, synthesis, certainty of evidence),
discussion, other information (registration, protocol, funding, competing interests,
data availability).

**ARRIVE** — reporting animal studies. Essential 10: study design, sample size,
inclusion/exclusion, randomization, blinding, outcome measures, statistical methods,
experimental animals, experimental procedures, results (plus a Recommended Set).

**SPIRIT** — clinical trial *protocols* (the planning document); paired with CONSORT
for the eventual report. Extensions: SPIRIT-Outcomes, SPIRIT-AI, SPIRIT-PRO. Key
sections: administrative info, introduction (background, objectives, design), methods
(participants/interventions/outcomes, sample size, recruitment, allocation, blinding,
data collection, data management, statistical methods, monitoring), ethics and
dissemination, appendices.

## Journal / venue format specifications

Target venue is a **PI-only decision**; once set, format the manuscript to that
venue's author guidelines. Durable, reusable venue specs live in one place so a
second manuscript for the same venue doesn't re-derive them. **Always re-check
against the current "Guide for Authors" at submission time** — journals revise limits
and requirements. Keep in-text `[Author, Year]` citations in the working draft
regardless of the venue's citation style; author-year vs. numeric rendering is an
export concern handled by the citation tooling.

*Example — Journal of Affective Disorders (Elsevier), original research:* Highlights
(3–5 bullets, **≤85 characters each**); **structured abstract ≤250 words** with bold
subheadings in order **Background / Methods / Results / Limitations / Conclusion**
(the *Limitations* subheading is mandatory and distinctive to JAD); ~6 keywords;
IMRaD body with a closing *Limitations* paragraph in the Discussion; author-year
(Harvard) citations with an alphabetical reference list; full-length ≤ ~5000 words and
**≤ 6 tables/figures combined**; **STROBE** for observational RWD (report cohort flow,
missingness, no causal-inference claim). When a project targets a new venue likely to
recur, add its spec following this pattern; project-specific application stays in the
project's guidance.
