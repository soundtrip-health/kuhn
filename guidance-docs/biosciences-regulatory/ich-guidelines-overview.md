# ICH Guidelines Overview: The Q, S, E, and M Series

> **Kuhn knowledge card.** Canonical source: https://www.ich.org/page/ich-guidelines (International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use). Source access/license: guidelines freely downloadable from the ICH website/database without registration; ICH text is copyrighted — summarize rather than reproduce. This card is a Kuhn-authored summary — cite and consult the canonical source for authoritative text.

## Scope

ICH develops harmonized technical guidelines for human pharmaceuticals so that
one data package can support registration across member regions (FDA, EMA,
PMDA, and a growing roster of regulators). The guidelines are organized into
four families — **Quality (Q), Safety (S), Efficacy (E), Multidisciplinary
(M)** — and, once finalized (Step 4) and adopted regionally (Step 5), they are
implemented as national/regional guidance (e.g., FDA republishes them as
Guidances for Industry). Any regulatory or clinical document written for
multi-region use should be framed against the applicable ICH guidelines.

## Key requirements

**Q — Quality** (chemistry, manufacturing, controls): Q1 stability testing;
Q2 validation of analytical procedures (paired with Q14 on analytical
procedure development); Q3A/Q3B/Q3C/Q3D impurities (substances, products,
residual solvents, elemental impurities); Q5 series biotechnological product
quality (viral safety, cell substrates, comparability); Q6A/Q6B
specifications; Q7 GMP for APIs; Q8 pharmaceutical development; Q9 quality
risk management; Q10 pharmaceutical quality system; Q11 drug substance
development and manufacture; Q12 lifecycle management; Q13 continuous
manufacturing.

**S — Safety** (nonclinical): S1 carcinogenicity; S2 genotoxicity; S3
toxicokinetics; S4 chronic toxicity duration; S5 reproductive/developmental
toxicity; S6 preclinical evaluation of biotechnology-derived products; S7A/S7B
safety pharmacology (including QT liability); S8 immunotoxicology; S9
nonclinical evaluation of anticancer pharmaceuticals; S10 photosafety; S11
nonclinical safety for pediatric development; S12 biodistribution of gene
therapy products.

**E — Efficacy** (clinical): E1 long-term safety exposure; E2A–E2F
pharmacovigilance (expedited reporting, PSURs/PBRERs, safety data management);
E3 structure and content of clinical study reports; E4 dose-response; E5
ethnic factors/foreign data; **E6 Good Clinical Practice** (E6(R3) current);
E7 geriatrics; E8 general considerations for clinical studies (E8(R1)); **E9
statistical principles** (with the E9(R1) estimands addendum); E10 choice of
control group; E11 pediatrics; E14 clinical QT evaluation; E17 multi-regional
clinical trials; E19 selective safety data collection; E20 adaptive designs
(in development).

**M — Multidisciplinary** (cross-cutting): M1 MedDRA terminology; M2
electronic transmission standards; M3 nonclinical studies timing to support
clinical trials; **M4 the Common Technical Document** (with M4Q/M4S/M4E); M7
mutagenic impurities; M8 eCTD; M10 bioanalytical method validation; M11
harmonized clinical protocol template (CeSHarP); M13 bioequivalence for
immediate-release solid oral forms.

**Numbering and versioning.** Codes are series letter + number, with
parenthesized revisions — E6(R2) → E6(R3) — and lettered sub-guidelines
(Q3A, S7B, E2A). Each guideline moves through a five-step process: Step 2
drafts are published for consultation; Step 4 is the finalized harmonized
text; Step 5 is regional implementation. Only Step 4/5 texts represent settled
expectations; Step 2 drafts must be flagged as drafts.

## How to apply when writing

- **Cite by code, revision, and step**: "ICH E9(R1)" not just "ICH statistics
  guideline." A usable pattern: *ICH Harmonised Guideline: Title. CODE(Rn).
  Step 4 version, date. Available at database.ich.org.* For FDA-facing
  documents, cite the FDA-adopted version.
- **Map document type to guideline before drafting**: clinical study reports
  → E3; protocols → E6(R3) and the M11 template; statistical analysis
  sections → E9 and the E9(R1) estimand framework; safety narratives and
  aggregate reports → E2 series; stability sections → Q1; method validation
  → Q2/M10; submission structure → M4/M8.
- **Check currency at database.ich.org** — revisions materially change
  expectations (e.g., E6(R3) restructured GCP around quality-by-design and
  risk proportionality; Q1 is under major revision).
- **Use ICH vocabulary** in regulatory prose: estimands and intercurrent
  events (E9(R1)), MedDRA terms for adverse events (M1), CTD section
  addresses (M4) — reviewers expect the harmonized terminology.
- **State conformance explicitly** where it matters ("conducted in accordance
  with ICH E6(R3) GCP") and justify any regional deviations, since regional
  implementation can add local requirements.

## Common pitfalls

- Citing a superseded revision (E6(R2) after R3 adoption, the retired E9
  statistics reading without the estimands addendum).
- Treating a Step 2 consultation draft as final harmonized guidance.
- Confusing series: M3 (nonclinical timing, multidisciplinary) vs S-series
  (nonclinical content); M4E (where clinical content goes) vs E3 (how a study
  report is written).
- Assuming ICH adoption is uniform — regional implementation (FDA guidance,
  EU adoption) can carry annexes, transition dates, or local additions.
- Writing "ICH-compliant" without naming which guidelines, which is
  unverifiable.
- Copying ICH text wholesale into internal documents rather than summarizing —
  ICH content is copyrighted, and verbatim reuse also masks whether the team
  understood it.
