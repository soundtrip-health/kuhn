# PRISMA 2020 — Reporting Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses

> **Kuhn knowledge card.** Canonical source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8005924/ (BMJ 2021; Page et al., PRISMA Group). Source access/license: free, open-access (CC BY); DOI 10.1136/bmj.n71; published simultaneously in BMJ, PLOS Medicine, and other journals. This card is a Kuhn-authored summary — cite and consult the canonical source for authoritative text.

## Scope

PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) 2020 defines what a published **systematic review** must report, with or without a meta-analysis. It primarily targets reviews of studies evaluating health interventions but is applicable to systematic reviews with other objectives (etiology, prevalence, diagnosis, prognosis) with minor adaptation. It is a *reporting* guideline, not a conduct manual (conduct is covered by, e.g., the Cochrane Handbook) and not a quality-appraisal tool (that is ROBIS/AMSTAR). PRISMA 2020 replaces PRISMA 2009. Family extensions exist for scoping reviews (PRISMA-ScR), protocols (PRISMA-P), searching (PRISMA-S), individual-participant-data reviews, and living systematic reviews.

## Key requirements

PRISMA 2020 comprises a **27-item main checklist**, a separate **abstract checklist** (12 items), and the **2020 flow diagram**. Paraphrased by section:

- **Title and abstract.** Identify the report as a systematic review in the title. The abstract follows its own checklist: objectives, eligibility criteria, information sources, risk-of-bias method, synthesis approach, number of included studies and participants, principal results with effect estimates, limitations of the evidence, interpretation, funding, and registration.
- **Introduction.** Rationale for the review in the context of existing knowledge, and explicit objectives or questions (typically framed as PICO: population, intervention, comparator, outcome).
- **Methods — eligibility.** Specify inclusion/exclusion criteria and how studies were grouped for the syntheses.
- **Methods — information sources and search.** List *all* information sources (databases, registers, websites, citation searching, contacting authors) with the date each was last searched, and present the full search strategy for every database, exactly as run.
- **Methods — study selection.** Describe the selection process: how many reviewers screened, whether independently, and any automation tools used.
- **Methods — data collection.** Describe the extraction process and every variable for which data were sought — outcomes, funding, and other characteristics — including assumptions made about missing or unclear information.
- **Methods — risk of bias.** Name the tool used to assess risk of bias in the included studies and how it was applied (reviewers, independence, automation).
- **Methods — effect measures.** State the effect measure(s) used for each outcome (e.g., risk ratio, mean difference).
- **Methods — synthesis.** Explain how studies were judged eligible for each synthesis; describe any data preparation (handling missing statistics, conversions) and how results were tabulated or displayed.
- **Methods — meta-analysis.** If meta-analysis was done, report the model (fixed/random effects), heterogeneity assessment, and software, plus investigations of heterogeneity (subgroup analysis, meta-regression) and sensitivity analyses.
- **Methods — reporting bias and certainty.** Describe methods for assessing bias due to missing results (publication/reporting bias) and how certainty of the body of evidence was assessed (typically GRADE).
- **Results — study selection.** Report the flow of records — identified, screened, excluded, retrieved, assessed, included — in the flow diagram, including citations for near-miss studies excluded at the full-text stage with reasons.
- **Results — studies and syntheses.** Present characteristics of included studies; risk-of-bias judgments per study; results of every individual study (summary statistics per group plus effect estimate and confidence interval, ideally in a table or forest plot); and results of each synthesis with heterogeneity statistics.
- **Results — additional analyses.** Report results of subgroup, sensitivity, and reporting-bias analyses, and certainty-of-evidence assessments per outcome.
- **Discussion.** Interpret results in the context of other evidence; discuss limitations of the *included evidence* and of the *review process itself*; state implications for practice, policy, and research.
- **Other information — registration and protocol.** Registration (e.g., PROSPERO) and where the protocol can be accessed, or a statement that the review was not registered; any amendments to the registered protocol.
- **Other information — funding and availability.** Funding and the role of funders; competing interests; and availability of data, analytic code, and other materials.

### The flow diagram

The 2020 diagram tracks records through four phases — identification, screening, eligibility assessment, inclusion — with separate accounting for databases/registers versus other sources (websites, organizations, citation searching), and boxes for records removed before screening (duplicates, automation-excluded). A variant adds a track for updates of previous reviews.

## How to apply when writing

- Freeze the search before drafting: record for each database the interface, coverage dates, the exact strategy string, and the run date; put full strategies in a supplement and cite it from Methods.
- Build the flow diagram from your screening log first; the arithmetic must be internally consistent (identified − removed − excluded = included) and match the text and abstract.
- Write Methods in the past tense against the checklist, one paragraph per cluster: eligibility, sources/search, selection, extraction, risk of bias, effect measures, synthesis, certainty.
- For every meta-analyzed outcome, report: number of studies and participants, pooled estimate with 95% CI, heterogeneity (e.g., I², tau²), the model used, and the GRADE certainty rating — a summary-of-findings table covers most of this.
- Distinguish clearly between limitations of the evidence base (few studies, high risk of bias, inconsistency) and limitations of your review methods (single-screener stages, language restrictions, no gray-literature search).
- If the review was registered, reconcile reported outcomes and methods with the PROSPERO record and explain deviations; if not registered, say so explicitly.
- Use the family extensions where they fit — PRISMA-P at protocol stage, PRISMA-S for the search report, PRISMA-ScR for scoping reviews — and cite which was followed.

## Common pitfalls

- Reporting only one database's search strategy, or an abbreviated "keywords included…" summary instead of the full reproducible strategy.
- Flow-diagram counts that do not add up, or full-text exclusions without stated reasons.
- Vote counting by statistical significance instead of a described synthesis method when meta-analysis is not possible.
- Omitting risk-of-bias results from the interpretation — pooled estimates presented as if all studies were reliable.
- Missing registration/protocol statement, data-availability statement, or funding/conflict declarations.
- Abstracts lacking effect estimates, the number of included studies, or a limitations sentence — the abstract has its own checklist.
- Using the 2009 diagram or checklist labels — journals expect the 2020 versions.
