# SPIRIT 2025 — Reporting Clinical Trial Protocols

> **Kuhn knowledge card.** Canonical source: https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(25)00770-6/fulltext (The Lancet / SPIRIT Collaboration). Source access/license: free, open-access (published simultaneously in The Lancet, Nature Medicine, PLOS Medicine). This card is a Kuhn-authored summary — cite and consult the canonical source for authoritative text.

## Scope

SPIRIT (Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials) 2025 defines the minimum content of a **clinical trial protocol** — the pre-trial document describing what will be done, not the post-trial results paper. It applies to protocols of randomized trials of any intervention type and is broadly usable for other interventional designs. SPIRIT 2025 replaces SPIRIT 2013 (34-item checklist plus the schedule-of-activities diagram). It is the protocol-stage sibling of CONSORT 2025: items are deliberately harmonized so that a SPIRIT-compliant protocol maps forward onto a CONSORT-compliant results report, and the two 2025 updates were developed together with shared terminology.

## Key requirements

The SPIRIT 2025 checklist (34 items, many with sub-items) organizes protocol content into the following clusters, paraphrased:

- **Administrative information.** Descriptive title identifying design and population; trial registry, registration number, and the full registration data set; protocol version and date with an amendment history.
- **Roles and oversight.** Funding sources and the roles of sponsor and funders; names, affiliations, and roles of protocol contributors; composition and responsibilities of oversight groups (steering committee, endpoint adjudication, data monitoring committee).
- **Open science (strengthened in 2025).** Plans for public access to the protocol and statistical analysis plan; the data-sharing plan for de-identified participant data and statistical code; declarations of interest; dissemination and authorship policy, including any publication restrictions.
- **Introduction.** Background and rationale, including a summary of relevant prior evidence and justification for the comparator choice; explicit objectives or hypotheses; trial design (framework — superiority, non-inferiority, equivalence — allocation ratio, and design type).
- **Methods — participants and setting.** Study settings and countries; eligibility criteria for participants (and for sites and providers where relevant); recruitment strategies.
- **Methods — interventions.** Interventions and comparators described per TIDieR in replicable detail, including criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions, adherence-improvement strategies, and permitted or prohibited concomitant care.
- **Methods — outcomes, timeline, sample size.** Primary and secondary outcomes fully specified (variable, analysis metric, aggregation, time point) with rationale; the participant timeline as the **schedule of enrolment, interventions, and assessments** diagram; sample size with the assumptions behind the calculation.
- **Methods — assignment and blinding.** Sequence generation (type of randomization, any stratification or blocking, who generates it), allocation-concealment mechanism, who enrolls and assigns; who will be blinded (participants, providers, outcome assessors, analysts) and the procedure for emergency unblinding.
- **Methods — data collection and management.** How data will be collected and by whom, instruments and their known validity, plans for retention and follow-up of participants who discontinue, and data-entry, coding, and storage systems with quality-control measures.
- **Methods — statistical analysis.** Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes — including the analysis population and the handling of intercurrent events and missing data (estimand-style framing, aligned with ICH E9(R1)) — plus any planned subgroup or adjusted analyses and interim analyses with stopping guidelines.
- **Methods — monitoring.** Whether a data monitoring committee exists (with charter reference) and why not if absent; harms definitions and the procedures for collecting, recording, and reporting adverse events; plans for auditing trial conduct.
- **Ethics.** Ethics-committee approval plan; how protocol amendments will be communicated to regulators, registries, and participants; who obtains informed consent and how (with model consent materials referenced); handling of biological specimens and ancillary studies; confidentiality protections.
- **Dissemination and post-trial provisions.** Post-trial care and compensation for trial-related harm; plans for communicating results to participants, clinicians, and the public.
- **Patient and public involvement (new emphasis in 2025).** How patients or the public were, or will be, involved in design, conduct, and dissemination.

### Changes vs SPIRIT 2013

SPIRIT 2025 adds two new items, revises five, and reorganizes the checklist to mirror CONSORT 2025:

- Open-science obligations (data/code sharing, protocol and SAP access) are promoted to explicit items.
- TIDieR intervention description is integrated directly into the interventions item.
- Outcome and analysis items adopt estimand language consistent with ICH E9(R1).
- Patient and public involvement becomes a checklist item in its own right.
- Guidance acknowledges newer trial features (decentralized elements, AI components via the SPIRIT-AI extension).

## How to apply when writing

- Use the checklist as the protocol's outline: most sponsors' protocol templates can be mapped item-by-item, and journals publishing protocols expect a completed SPIRIT checklist with page references.
- Build the schedule-of-activities table (visits × procedures/assessments, with time windows) early; every assessment mentioned in Methods must appear in it, and vice versa.
- Specify each outcome with all four elements — measurement variable, analysis metric (e.g., change from baseline), aggregation (e.g., mean difference, proportion), and time point — so the future results paper cannot be accused of outcome switching.
- Write the sample-size paragraph so a statistician could reproduce it: effect size assumed and its justification, variance assumptions, alpha, power, allocation ratio, and inflation for attrition.
- Keep registry, protocol, and SAP mutually consistent; version the protocol and log every amendment with date, change, and rationale.
- Pre-state the estimand: population, treatment condition, endpoint, handling of intercurrent events, and population-level summary — this is what regulators and CONSORT 2025 will ask for later.

## Common pitfalls

- Protocols that describe interventions less precisely than the eligibility criteria — missing dose-modification rules, delivery details, or adherence plans.
- Outcomes named but not operationally defined (no metric, aggregation method, or time point).
- No allocation-concealment mechanism described, or blinding claimed without an emergency-unblinding procedure.
- Missing or boilerplate data-monitoring and harms sections (no AE definitions, timelines, or reporting routes).
- Omitting the amendment-communication plan, data-sharing plan, or dissemination/authorship policy.
- Inconsistencies between the protocol, the registry entry, and the consent documents.
