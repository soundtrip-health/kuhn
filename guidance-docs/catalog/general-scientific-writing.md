# Scientific Writing & Publishing Knowledge Library

This curated collection brings together 18 authoritative, freely accessible resources covering general scientific writing and publishing best practices. Resources span authorship standards, research reporting, data management, citation formats, writing clarity, research ethics, and peer review guidance from respected international organizations, universities, and government agencies.

---

## Authorship, Publication Standards & Guidelines

### ICMJE Recommendations
**Authority:** International Committee of Medical Journal Editors  
**URL:** https://www.icmje.org/  
**Format:** HTML (browsable), PDF downloadable  
**Access/License:** Free, public domain  
**Description:** Comprehensive guidance on defining author roles, contributor responsibilities, manuscript preparation standards, disclosure of conflicts of interest, and emerging issues like AI in publishing. Updated annually; essential reference for medical, biomedical, and health sciences publishing. Includes searchable navigation and multiple language translations.

### ICMJE Authorship Criteria & CRediT Framework
**Authority:** International Committee of Medical Journal Editors  
**URL:** https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html  
**Format:** HTML  
**Access/License:** Free, public domain  
**Description:** Details the four criteria for authorship (conception/design, data acquisition/analysis/interpretation, drafting/critical review, final approval and accountability) and guidance on acknowledging non-author contributors. Addresses author order, ghost authorship, and accountability standards.

## Research Reporting Guidelines

### EQUATOR Network (Enhancing QUAlity and Transparency Of health Research)
**Authority:** International collaborative research transparency initiative  
**URL:** https://www.equator-network.org/  
**Format:** HTML, PDF checklists and tools  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** Central hub providing over 250 reporting guidelines tailored to different study types (trials, observational studies, qualitative research, systematic reviews). Includes searchable guideline library, training courses (EQUATOR Publication School), toolkits for journal implementation, and practical guidance. Supported by major publishers and funders including BMJ, The Lancet, and NIHR.

### STROBE Statement (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology)
**Authority:** International initiative of epidemiologists, methodologists, and journal editors  
**URL:** https://www.strobe-statement.org/  
**Format:** PDF, Word formats, multiple languages  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** 22-item checklist and explanation document for reporting observational studies (cohort, case–control, cross-sectional). Provides detailed guidance per section (title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion). Available on EQUATOR Network, PLoS Medicine, Annals of Internal Medicine, and Epidemiology.

### CONSORT Statement (CONsolidated Standards OF Reporting Trials)
**Authority:** CONSORT Group (international collaboration of researchers, statisticians, editors)  
**URL:** https://www.consort-spirit.org/  
**Format:** PDF, editable checklist, flow diagram  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** 30-item checklist and flow diagram for reporting randomized controlled trials. Addresses trial design, analysis, and interpretation. Includes CONSORT 2025 updates, Explanation and Elaboration document, and complementary SPIRIT guidelines for trial protocols. Includes COBWEB editing tool and SEPTRE protocol tool.

### PRISMA Statement (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses)
**Authority:** PRISMA Group (international methodologists and researchers)  
**URL:** https://www.prisma-statement.org/  
**Format:** PDF checklist, flow diagram, Word documents  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** Reporting guideline for systematic reviews and meta-analyses. PRISMA 2020 is the current standard (updated from 2009 version). Includes checklist, Explanation and Elaboration document, and interactive flow diagram. Extensions available for specific review types (abstracts, diagnostic accuracy, equity, protocols, searching).

## Scientific Writing Style & Clarity

### Stanford University: Writing in the Sciences (Coursera Course)
**Authority:** Stanford University, taught by Dr. Kristin Sainani  
**URL:** https://www.coursera.org/learn/sciwrite  
**Format:** Video course (8 modules)  
**Access/License:** Free audit option or paid certificate; some courses offer financial aid  
**Description:** Eight-module course covering principles of clear scientific writing, sentence-level editing, scientific manuscript format, peer review, grant writing, ethical issues, and communicating to general audiences. 612,000+ enrollees with 4.9-star rating. Emphasizes clarity over complexity with practical editing exercises.

### Stanford: A Guide to Quantitative Scientific Writing
**Authority:** Stanford University Global Health Program  
**URL:** https://globalhealth.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/A-Guide-to-Quantitative-Scientific-Writing-V-23.pdf  
**Format:** PDF  
**Access/License:** Free, openly available  
**Description:** Practical guide for writing research articles incorporating quantitative methods and statistical findings. Covers clear communication of numbers, statistical concepts, and data-driven narratives. Emphasizes avoiding jargon and explaining concepts as if to a layperson.

### George Mason University Writing Center: IMRaD Format Guide
**Authority:** George Mason University  
**URL:** https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/writing-resources/imrad/writing-an-imrad-report  
**Format:** HTML  
**Access/License:** Free, publicly available  
**Description:** Comprehensive guidance on the standard Introduction-Methods-Results-Discussion structure for scientific articles. Explains purpose and conventions for each section, including appropriate tense, voice, and content placement. Identifies common pitfalls and provides practical examples.

### CDC Plain Language Guidance
**Authority:** U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention  
**URL:** https://www.cdc.gov/other/plainwriting.html  
**Format:** HTML, downloadable resources  
**Access/License:** Public domain (U.S. federal material)  
**Description:** Federal guidance on plain language communication for scientific and health information. Aligns with Plain Writing Act of 2010. Provides tools for assessing public communication clarity using CDC's Clear Communication Index. Includes Everyday Words for Public Health Communication resource.

### NIH Plain Language Initiative
**Authority:** U.S. National Institutes of Health Clinical Center  
**URL:** https://www.cc.nih.gov/plain-language  
**Format:** HTML  
**Access/License:** Public domain  
**Description:** NIH guidance on achieving health literacy through plain language approaches. Part of the Clear Communication initiative promoting accessible scientific communication. Includes training resources and best practices for federal health research communication.

## Citation & Referencing Standards

### APA Citation Style Guide
**Authority:** American Psychological Association  
**URL:** https://apastyle.apa.org/  
**Format:** HTML, PDF resources, examples  
**Access/License:** Free resources and tutorials available; full style guide purchase available  
**Description:** Standard citation format for education, psychology, and social sciences. Emphasizes publication dates and author accountability. Includes formatting for in-text citations, references, and proper attribution. Wide institutional support and free online tutorials.

### Chicago Manual of Style Citation Guide
**Authority:** University of Chicago Press  
**URL:** https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/  
**Format:** HTML, PDF  
**Access/License:** Free online resources; full manual for purchase  
**Description:** Citation standard for humanities, history, and business. Offers two systems: notes-and-bibliography (for humanities) and author-date (for sciences and social sciences). Widely adopted and particularly valued for clarity in complex citation situations.

### Library Citation Guides (APA, MLA, Chicago, IEEE Comparison)
**Authority:** University of Pittsburgh University Library; Brown University Library; multiple institutions  
**URL:** https://pitt.libguides.com/citationhelp (and similar at https://libguides.brown.edu/citations/styles)  
**Format:** HTML guides, comparison charts  
**Access/License:** Free, publicly available  
**Description:** Institutional library guides providing free comparison and guidance for multiple citation styles (APA, MLA, Chicago, IEEE, Turabian). Includes step-by-step examples, downloadable quick-reference guides, and links to official style resources. Maintained by university librarians.

## Research Ethics, Integrity & Publication Standards

### COPE: Committee on Publication Ethics
**Authority:** International non-profit, 15,000+ members worldwide  
**URL:** https://publicationethics.org/  
**Format:** HTML, PDF guidelines  
**Access/License:** Free Core Practices and guidance documents  
**Description:** Leading resource for research and publication ethics. Provides guidance on authorship disputes, research misconduct, plagiarism, conflict of interest, and ethical conduct for editors, publishers, and peer reviewers. Includes COPE Core Practices (consolidated ethics framework) and Ethical Guidelines for Peer Reviewers. Offers free eLearning course in publication ethics.

### COPE: Ethical Guidelines for Peer Reviewers
**Authority:** Committee on Publication Ethics  
**URL:** https://publicationethics.org/guidance/guideline/ethical-guidelines-peer-reviewers  
**Format:** HTML, PDF  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** Standards for conduct during peer review process applicable across disciplines. Addresses confidentiality, expertise requirements, conflict of interest, timeliness, and professional conduct. Serves as educational resource for institutions training researchers and reviewers.

### Frontiers Peer Review Guidelines
**Authority:** Frontiers (open-access publisher)  
**URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/guidelines/peer-review-guidelines  
**Format:** HTML  
**Access/License:** Free, publicly available  
**Description:** Comprehensive peer review standards emphasizing manuscript quality and validity over perceived impact. Details single-anonymized model, quality acceptance criteria, and interactive review process. Outlines reviewer responsibilities, editor roles, and dispute resolution mechanisms.

### Disclosure of Conflicts of Interest in Scientific Publications
**Authority:** Research literature (NIH/PubMed Central)  
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7819374/  
**Format:** PDF, HTML  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** Guidance on identifying, reporting, and managing financial and non-financial conflicts of interest. Over 90% of scientific journals mandate disclosure. Details what must be disclosed (within 5 years), ICMJE conflict disclosure form, and implementation in published articles. Essential for publication integrity.

## Data Management & Reproducibility

### NIH Scientific Data Management and Sharing Policy
**Authority:** U.S. National Institutes of Health Office of Science Policy  
**URL:** https://osp.od.nih.gov/policies/scientific-data-management-policy/  
**Format:** HTML, PDF guidance documents  
**Access/License:** Public domain  
**Description:** Federal requirement (effective January 2023) for NIH-supported research to develop and implement data management and sharing plans. Addresses minimum standards for data sharing, repository selection, metadata, and timing. Includes best practices for maximizing policy compliance and research transparency. Supports research reproducibility and integrity.

### NIH Training on Rigor and Reproducibility
**Authority:** U.S. National Institutes of Health, Grants & Funding office  
**URL:** https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/reproducibility/training  
**Format:** HTML, linked training resources  
**Access/License:** Free, publicly available  
**Description:** Comprehensive training resources on research reproducibility, research design, data management, and open science practices. Includes desirable characteristics for data repositories, guidance on unique persistent identifiers for datasets, and metadata standards. Supports institutional capacity building in research rigor.

## Researcher Identification & Open Access

### ORCID (Open Researcher and Contributor ID)
**Authority:** ORCID, Inc. (international non-profit)  
**URL:** https://orcid.org/  
**Format:** Web-based registration and profile  
**Access/License:** Free, perpetual registration  
**Description:** Unique 16-digit persistent identifier for researchers. Registration takes <2 minutes and is free for life. Survives name changes, institutional moves, and career transitions. Enables accurate attribution of publications, connects with institutional repositories and funding systems. Increasingly required by publishers, funders, and institutions.

### Open Access Repositories and Institutional Publishing
**Authority:** Multiple institutions, funders, and international initiatives  
**URL:** https://open-access.network/en/information/publishing/repositories (and institutional guides)  
**Format:** HTML guides, repository platforms  
**Access/License:** Free deposit and access (most institutional and open repositories)  
**Description:** Guide to depositing and accessing scholarly work through institutional repositories and subject-specific open access repositories. Explains repository types, open access delivery models, and how repositories complement peer-reviewed journal publication. Most open repositories are managed by institutions, funders, or non-profits at no cost to depositors.

## Grant Writing & Proposal Development

### NSF Proposal Writing Guidelines
**Authority:** U.S. National Science Foundation  
**URL:** https://www.nsf.gov/publications/pub_summ.jsp?ods_key=nsf24052 (current NSF Proposal Guide)  
**Format:** PDF, HTML documentation  
**Access/License:** Public domain (U.S. federal material)  
**Description:** Official requirements and guidance for NSF grant proposals, including Project Summary structure (Overview, Intellectual Merit, Broader Impacts), Project Description specifications (15 pages standard), data management plan requirements, and budget guidelines. Updated annually with current program solicitations. Essential for federal STEM funding applications.

## Summary

These 18 resources represent the most authoritative and freely accessible guidance in scientific writing and publishing. They cover essential competencies including authorship and contributor roles, research reporting standards tailored to study design, data management and research integrity, clear scientific communication, citation standards, ethical publication practices, and peer review principles. All resources are freely available through official websites or institutional repositories, making them suitable for integration into scientific writing training, organizational reference libraries, and educational curricula across disciplines.
