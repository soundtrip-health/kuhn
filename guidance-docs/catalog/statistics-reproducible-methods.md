# Scientific Knowledge Library: Statistics, Data Science & Reproducible Research

A curated collection of authoritative, freely accessible resources on statistical reporting, reproducible research methods, data management, and open science practices for scientific writers and researchers.

---

## Statistical Reporting Guidelines

### SAMPL Guidelines: Basic Statistical Reporting for Biomedical Journals
**Authority:** Lang TA, Altman DG  
**Publisher:** International Journal of Nursing Studies  
**URL:** https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/sampl/  
**Format:** PDF (downloadable checklist)  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** Foundational guidelines for reporting statistical methods and analyses in biomedical research, covering descriptive statistics, inference, study design, and results presentation to improve transparency and reproducibility in health journals.

### The ASA Statement on Statistical Significance and P-Values
**Authority:** American Statistical Association  
**Publisher:** The American Statistician (2016); updated 2019  
**URL:** https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Real_World/ASA_statement.pdf  
**Format:** PDF  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** Official statement cautioning against misuse of p-values and statistical significance testing, recommending effect sizes, Bayesian methods, and confidence intervals for more comprehensive statistical reporting.

### CONSORT 2025 Statement: Randomized Controlled Trials
**Authority:** CONSORT Group  
**Publisher:** JAMA, The Lancet, BMJ, Nature Medicine, PLoS Medicine (simultaneous 2025)  
**URL:** https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/consort/  
**Format:** Checklist (30 items) + explanation document  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** Updated standards for reporting parallel group randomized trials with 30-item checklist covering study design, implementation, analysis, results, and discussion to ensure trial transparency and reproducibility.

### STROBE Guidelines: Observational Studies in Epidemiology
**Authority:** STROBE Initiative  
**Publisher:** PLoS Medicine, Annals of Internal Medicine, Epidemiology (2007)  
**URL:** https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/strobe/  
**Format:** 22-item checklist + explanation and elaboration  
**Access/License:** Free, open access (published in multiple journals)  
**Description:** Reporting guidelines for cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional observational studies, addressing design, methods, results, and interpretation to enhance clarity and reduce bias in epidemiological research.

### Guide to Effect Sizes and Confidence Intervals
**Authority:** Matthew B. Jane  
**Publisher:** Educational resource (web-based guide)  
**URL:** https://matthewbjane.quarto.pub/guide-to-effect-sizes-and-confidence-intervals/  
**Format:** Interactive web guide + downloadable PDF  
**Access/License:** Free, open access  
**Description:** Comprehensive guide on computing, interpreting, and reporting effect sizes and confidence intervals with practical examples, following American Psychological Association standards for modern statistical communication.

## Reproducibility & Open Science Standards

### The FAIR Guiding Principles for Scientific Data Management and Stewardship
**Authority:** Wilkinson MD et al.  
**Publisher:** Nature Scientific Data (2016)  
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201618  
**Format:** Open access journal article  
**Access/License:** Creative Commons Attribution (open access)  
**Description:** Foundation for making research data Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable through machine-actionable standards, enabling data discovery and reuse by both humans and computational systems.

### Transparency and Openness Promotion (TOP) Guidelines
**Authority:** Center for Open Science  
**Publisher:** TOP Advisory Board (updated 2025)  
**URL:** https://www.cos.io/initiatives/top-guidelines  
**Format:** Full guidelines + implementation resources  
**Access/License:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0)  
**Description:** Policy framework for advancing open science across eight standards including study preregistration, data/code/materials sharing, and verification practices, with recommended language for journal adoption and institutional implementation.

### Reproducibility Project: Psychology Methodology
**Authority:** Open Science Collaboration  
**Publisher:** Science (2015); associated resources  
**URL:** https://www.cos.io/blog/research/reproducibility-project-psychology  
**Format:** Published study + methods documentation  
**Access/License:** Open access  
**Description:** Large-scale empirical study replicating 100 published psychology findings, establishing rigorous replication methodology and documenting challenges in reproducing published results, informing current reproducibility standards.

## Data Visualization & Communication

### Best Practices for Data Visualisation
**Authority:** Royal Statistical Society  
**Publisher:** Royal Statistical Society Data Visualization Group (2023)  
**URL:** https://royal-statistical-society.github.io/datavisguide/  
**Format:** Online guide with code examples + downloadable PDF  
**Access/License:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0)

## Research Design & Preregistration

### Open Science Framework Preregistration
**Authority:** Center for Open Science  
**Publisher:** Open Science Framework  
**URL:** https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg  
**Format:** Online platform + templates (PDF checklists)  
**Access/License:** Free platform; templates openly licensed  
**Description:** Comprehensive preregistration system with 10+ validated templates (general, secondary data, qualitative, registered reports) enabling researchers to prospectively specify hypotheses, methods, and analysis plans with citable DOIs.

### Registered Reports Publishing Model
**Authority:** Center for Open Science / Adopting Journals  
**Publisher:** Multiple journals (Cognitive Science, eLife, Psychological Science, etc.)  
**URL:** https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports  
**Format:** Publishing guidelines + template submission protocols  
**Access/License:** Free resources  
**Description:** Publishing model separating exploratory from confirmatory research through Stage 1 peer review of methods before data collection, with provisional acceptance reducing publication bias and incentivizing methodological rigor.

## Software Citation & Code Availability

### Software Citation Principles
**Authority:** FORCE11 Software Citation Working Group  
**Publisher:** PeerJ Preprints (2016); distributed via multiple venues  
**URL:** https://force11.org/post/software-citation-principles/  
**Format:** PDF + accompanying materials  
**Access/License:** Creative Commons Attribution (open access)  
**Description:** Six principles (Importance, Credit, Attribution, Accessibility, Specificity, Persistence) establishing consistent practices for citing research software across disciplines, enabling software recognition and reproducibility.

### Open Source Software and Code Guidelines
**Authority:** UNESCO / Research Software Alliance  
**Publisher:** UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science (2021)  
**URL:** https://www.researchsoft.org/resource/guidelines/  
**Format:** Policy documentation + technical guidelines  
**Access/License:** Open access  
**Description:** Standards for making research code and software publicly available under open licenses (GPL, MIT, Apache), including documentation, build parameters, and input files necessary for reproducibility and reuse.

## Data Management & Sharing

### NIH Data Management and Sharing Policy
**Authority:** National Institutes of Health  
**Publisher:** NIH Office of Intramural Research (effective 2023)  
**URL:** https://oir.nih.gov/sourcebook/intramural-program-oversight/intramural-data-sharing/2023-nih-data-management-sharing-policy  
**Format:** Policy documentation + toolkit  
**Access/License:** Government public domain  
**Description:** Mandate requiring NIH-funded researchers to plan, budget for, and implement data management and sharing, with data sharing no later than publication or end of funding period, supported by free DMPTool for plan development.

### Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions
**Authority:** Cochrane Collaboration  
**Publisher:** Cochrane Library (6th edition, 2024)  
**URL:** https://www.cochrane.org/learn  
**Format:** Online handbook + downloadable sections  
**Access/License:** Free (introductory resources); advanced resources for members  
**Description:** Comprehensive methods guide for conducting systematic reviews with transparent, reproducible methodology including protocol registration, risk of bias assessment, synthesis approaches, and reporting standards adopted across health research.

## Sample Size, Power & Reporting Quality

### Power Analysis and Sample Size Reporting Guidelines
**Authority:** Multiple authors (comprehensive review)  
**Publisher:** Journal articles and educational resources  
**URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10888683241228328  
**Format:** Journal article + supplementary methods  
**Access/License:** Open access or institutional access  
**Description:** Standards for reporting a priori power calculations including effect size justification, error rates, and variability estimates, ensuring adequate statistical power disclosure and preventing underpowered studies in confirmatory research.
