## Antidepressant prescribing in PTSD

Clinicians **routinely prescribe well beyond the two FDA-approved SSRIs (sertraline
and paroxetine)** for PTSD, encompassing SNRIs, other SSRIs, atypical antidepressants,
TCAs, and adjunctive agents — supported by guideline recommendations and real-world
prescribing data.

**Guideline-recommended agents beyond FDA-approved SSRIs.** VA/DoD 2023 CPG recommends
paroxetine, sertraline, and venlafaxine (SNRI) first-line (fluoxetine downgraded to
"insufficient evidence"). APA 2025 CPG conditionally recommends fluoxetine, paroxetine,
sertraline, venlafaxine. AAFP/Cochrane 2022: moderate-certainty for SSRIs,
low-certainty for mirtazapine and amitriptyline. NICE: trauma-focused CBT first-line,
with SSRIs and venlafaxine as pharmacotherapy options. Cochrane 2026: benefit for
SSRIs, venlafaxine, mirtazapine, amitriptyline; antipsychotics (risperidone,
olanzapine) on secondary outcomes. Zhang et al. 2023 NMA: significant reduction with
paroxetine, venlafaxine, fluoxetine, sertraline, and the atypical antipsychotics
quetiapine/risperidone/olanzapine.

**Real-world prescribing.** The Holder et al. 2021 national VA study (1.35M veterans,
2009–2018) found SSRIs/SNRIs most common but tracked substantial atypical-antipsychotic,
mirtazapine, prazosin, trazodone, TCA, and non-benzodiazepine-hypnotic use (over the
decade SSRI prescribing fell 12.3% absolute, SNRI rose 6.4%, atypical antipsychotics
fell 5.4%). Shalev et al. 2017 (NEJM): 74% of affected war veterans receive
pharmacotherapy, 89% of those antidepressants, 61% anxiolytics/sedative-hypnotics, 34%
antipsychotics. Fortney et al. 2025 (JAMA Psychiatry): ~66.3% receive an SSRI/SNRI in
the first year after diagnosis. Bajor et al. 2022 algorithm sequences: sleep-targeted
agents (prazosin, trazodone, hydroxyzine) → an SSRI → a second SSRI or venlafaxine →
second-generation antipsychotics for augmentation.

**Key limitations in the evidence.** Real-world data is heavily VA-weighted;
community/private-practice patterns are less characterized. Off-label rates for
specific non-SSRI agents (bupropion, trazodone as monotherapy, duloxetine) are poorly
quantified. Generalizability to military populations is limited even within the
guideline base. The non-SSRI evidence base is substantially thinner than for SSRIs
(Cochrane found no RCTs reporting efficacy for SNRIs on the primary outcome, though
venlafaxine showed benefit on secondary CAPS measures; mirtazapine and amitriptyline
were low-certainty).

## PTSD outcome instruments — CAPS-5, PCL-5, and bridging

Two instruments recur in ketamine/esketamine PTSD work. **CAPS-5**
(Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5) is the **FDA gold standard** — a
clinician-administered structured interview, what registrational PTSD trials use as the
primary endpoint. **PCL-5** (PTSD Checklist for DSM-5) is a **patient-reported** 20-item
self-report (0–80) — what routine practice and EHRs capture at scale, while CAPS-5 is
rarely present in real-world data. That gap is the central measurement problem in PTSD
RWE.

**Regulatory posture.** FDA **does permit PCL-5** in long-term studies **if patient
selection is rigorous** — the RWE-relevant opening to demote a weaker instrument to a
validated secondary surface rather than lean on it as primary (the "fit-for-purpose
endpoint" lever). The general move for a weak-but-available instrument is to establish
fitness by **correlation against the accepted instrument** — published concordance for a
retrospective base, or an in-program crosswalk (independent-rater CAPS-5 in a
calibration subset alongside routine PCL-5) for a prospective registry.

**PCL-5 ↔ CAPS-5 concordance — commonly cited magnitudes** (for scoping/bridging
arguments): cross-sectional correlation ≈ 0.7; response-delta correlation ≈ 0.88;
Abdallah 2021 is a useful VA/active-duty CAPS-5 anchor. **These numbers must be sourced
before entering prose** — they are scoping approximations, not constants (the exact
value depends on population and study). Verify and register the specific values before
any draft cites them; flag with `[TODO: verify: ...]` until then. Do not smooth over
instrument divergence, watch for anchor trials using a third instrument (e.g. a 24-hour
modified IES-R), and calibrate claims to the statistic — a ~0.7 correlation is
*moderate-to-strong*, not "excellent agreement."
